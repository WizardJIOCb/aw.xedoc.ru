# Original colony creatures

Three original stylized enemies were authored with Blender 5.1 for this
prototype. Their shapes, materials and animation curves are generated by
`scripts/build-creatures.py`; no AWPlanet artwork or external animal meshes,
textures or animation assets are used.

| Model                        | Geometry        | Rig       | Material primitives | File size     |
| ---------------------------- | --------------- | --------- | ------------------- | ------------- |
| `public/assets/chicken.glb`  | 2,478 triangles | 7 joints  | 5                   | 145,832 bytes |
| `public/assets/rat.glb`      | 3,016 triangles | 10 joints | 6                   | 197,488 bytes |
| `public/assets/scorpion.glb` | 3,788 triangles | 16 joints | 5                   | 266,092 bytes |

Every model contains one glTF mesh and one skin. Three.js creates a skinned
render primitive for each material, so a chicken/scorpion uses five draw calls
and a rat six, rather than one call for every anatomical piece. Texture files
are unnecessary: all colours, roughness and metalness are material values.

## Anatomy

- Chicken: feathered body and breast, neck and head, four comb lobes, wattle,
  beak, black eyes, articulated wings with flight feathers, tail feathers,
  two shins and three-toed feet.
- Rat: gray body and pale underside, tapered snout and pink nose, round ears
  with inner skin, black eyes, whiskers, two incisors, four legs and paws with
  toes, tapered segmented tail.
- Scorpion: head shield, five abdomen plates, six small eyes, **eight legs**,
  two articulated claw arms with hooked fingers, segmented curved tail,
  venom bulb and dark stinger.

The joined geometry retains rigid normalized vertex weights for each animated
part. Legs, head, wings/claws and tail sections have their own bones.

## Animation and scene integration

All three files use exactly these native animation names:

| Clip     | Duration | Intended playback              |
| -------- | -------- | ------------------------------ |
| `Idle`   | 2.0 s    | Loop                           |
| `Walk`   | 1.0 s    | Loop                           |
| `Attack` | 0.8 s    | Play once, return to Idle/Walk |

Idle includes breathing/head motion or tail movement. Walk steps the legs;
Attack pecks/flaps for the chicken, lunges for the rat, and moves claws and
tail for the scorpion. Animations stay in place; gameplay owns world movement.

Coordinate convention is glTF **Y-up**, metres, facing **+Z**. Origins are
centred at ground level. Measured unscaled rest bounds:

| Model    | Width X | Height Y | Depth Z, including tail/beak |
| -------- | ------- | -------- | ---------------------------- |
| Chicken  | 0.567 m | 0.862 m  | 0.905 m                      |
| Rat      | 0.727 m | 0.506 m  | 1.725 m                      |
| Scorpion | 1.458 m | 1.030 m  | 1.553 m                      |

The rat tail accounts for much of its depth. Normalize by desired body height
or a manually selected species scale, rather than total bounding-box length.
Use `SkeletonUtils.clone` for separate enemies and a dedicated AnimationMixer
per clone. Play `Attack` with `THREE.LoopOnce`, then resume the movement clip.

## Rebuild and verification

```powershell
& 'C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.1\blender.exe' `
  --background --python '.\scripts\build-creatures.py'
```

The script exports the three GLBs, imports those final GLBs into a fresh scene,
renders `public/assets/creatures-preview.png`, and saves the editable scene as
`source-art/creatures.blend`. The final render was visually inspected: all
three species are recognizable and their limbs/claws/tail are present.

Three.js loaded every final GLB and sampled each clip at five points. All
skinned bounds remained finite; maximum animated extent was 0.925 m for the
chicken, 1.740 m for the rat, and 1.854 m for the scorpion. The detailed result
is `public/assets/creatures-check.json`. This validates native asset motion;
browser gameplay and combat-event synchronization are checked separately.
